# AXF-129 — Pagamento por terceiro sem reembolso automático

- **Jira:** https://axon-personal-finances.atlassian.net/browse/AXF-129 (parent AXF-1; split from AXF-5)
- **Branch:** `feature/AXF-129-no-automatic-debt` (base `origin/sprint/sprint-4` @ `337394d`).
- **Siblings already delivered and extended, not rebuilt:** AXF-126 (`propose`/`confirm`/`discard`,
  guard, integrity reads, controller), AXF-127 (derived singular projection) and AXF-128
  (neutralization of the counter-entries in the consolidated view, `reverse.v1`).

> Two rules only: (1) confirming the economic allocation of a third-party payment must not generate
> debt — no question, no debtor/creditor review, no reimbursement obligation, no balance, no partial
> settlement, no due date — so the aggregate only ever expresses a positive share of a positive
> movement and the guard refuses any amount beside or beyond it; (2) a transfer between accounts
> stays two distinct movements and is never renamed into one allocation or one refund, and it
> duplicates no revenue/expense in the consolidated totals. No new object, field, permission set,
> Custom Label or metadata container.

## AC-1 → implementation

**The confirmation writes nothing but the set and its parcels.** `confirm()` moves the set to
`CONFIRMED`, stamps `EffectiveAt`, takes the single active token and derives the singular projection;
the parcels are written by `propose()` and never edited. There is no counter-entry record, no
obligation record and no review item anywhere in that path — FR-50's counter-entries exist only
inside a consolidation calculation, as in-memory `Contribution` values keyed by account, currency and
source. A derived value cannot become a debt because nothing persists it.

**Absence is asserted against the ratified vocabulary, not against intuition.** The test enumerates
the custom fields of `EAS` and `EA`, the restricted values of `AXF_RVI_PKL_Type__c` and the accepted
types of `ALT_CLS_ReviewQueueService`, and fails if any of them names a debt, an obligation, a
balance, a due date, a partial settlement or a debtor/creditor review. It then serializes the
confirmed `SetView` and its `ShareView` and compares the member sets **exactly** with the ratified §5
read surface, so a later `dueDate`, `owedBy` or `settlementBalance` added to what the caller receives
fails here. Positive controls (the aggregate really has custom fields; the queue really has types;
the view really has members) keep the absence claims from being vacuous.

**And the shape that could manufacture one is refused.** The only way the aggregate could express an
amount someone owes is an amount that does not fit the movement it claims to share — a balance
standing beside the fact — or a set that carries no movement at all. `confirm()` would already reject
both through exact 100% and magnitude conservation, but the guard is the content fence for writers
that are _already inside_ the service window (`beginWrite()`), which is exactly the class's declared
purpose; it refuses them earlier, and the test proves the refusal inside that window.

## AC-2 → implementation

**Distinct movements.** The test builds a transfer as AXF-32 defines it: a `DEBIT` fact on the
origin's bank account and a `CREDIT` fact on the destination's, each with its own source key, its own
account and its own amount. Nothing links them — this story creates no `InternalTransfer` and no
neutrality key — and both stay visible in their own accounts.

**Not a renamed refund, and no duplicated revenue/expense.** Consolidating both holders returns
`inflow` 500, `outflow` 500, `net` 0 and `factCount` 2: each leg contributes exactly once, at its own
holder, and the pair is neutral, so the transfer duplicates nothing. The aggregate cannot become the
pair: `EAS` anchors to exactly one movement, which the test proves both positively (the allocation of
the paid leg anchors to that leg and to nothing else) and negatively (a set carrying two anchors is
refused by `EAS_VR_AnchorExclusive`).

**The distinction between the two cases.** A third-party payment is an _economic attribution of one
movement_: the destination assumes the leg it received, the consolidated view shows that leg as
`ALLOCATION` for the destination and the received leg as `HOLDER` for the same destination, the totals
stay at 500/500/0, and the other leg keeps its magnitude, its identity and no set of its own. The
difference in holders is expressed as a share — never as a receivable, a payable or an obligation.

## Judgement calls and residuals

- **The deliverable was chosen from evidence, not from the story's vocabulary.** Before writing
  anything the question was whether a debt concept exists to remove, or a missing guard exists to add.
  It does not exist to remove: §5's field list has no debt, obligation, balance, settlement or
  due-date field, no object in this branch carries one, and the counter-entries of FR-50 are never
  persisted. So the honest deliverable is (b) assertions that pin the absence — the field, question
  and read-surface vocabulary checks — plus (a) one small enforcing guard on the only reachable shape
  that could manufacture an obligation from the aggregate, and (c) the AC-2 distinction proven end to
  end. No speculative machinery: no object, field, permission set, label, config flag or new class
  besides the test class.
- **The guard rules are contract-true, not invented.** "Allocation sempre positiva" (§3) plus the
  exact-100% confirmation already imply that every parcel fits inside its fact; the guard states that
  invariant where the content of the aggregate is decided, so a writer that bypasses the service
  cannot reach a state the confirmation would refuse. No legitimate writer is affected: `propose()`
  derives magnitudes from percentages ≤ 100 with the residue going to the largest share, `reverse()`
  writes exactly the fact magnitude, and the test's positive control re-proves both.
- **The XOR validation rule belongs to AXF-126 and was only asserted, never changed.** It is the
  structural reason an allocation can never embody a transfer pair, which is AC-2's own claim, so
  asserting it here is evidence rather than scope creep; the rule itself is untouched and had no test
  before this pass.
- **Nothing was absorbed from AXF-32 or AXF-41.** `AXF_OBJ_InternalTransfer__c` was not created, no
  neutrality key was introduced, no transfer detection or suggestion was added, and the card
  chargeback/credit/dispute slice (AXF-41) is untouched: `ALT_CLS_TransactionObservationService`'s
  reversal classification and `ALT_CLS_SourceLinkService`'s role refusal are unchanged.
- **PENDENCY — the transfer's own neutralization is not implemented here, and must not be.** In a
  scope that holds only one side of a transfer, that side still contributes its own leg: the netting
  of the pair is AXF-32's contract (`AXF_ITR_EXI_NeutralityKey__c`), and AXF-32 is blocked by AXF-31
  and not in this branch. This pass deliberately proves only that the transfer is not _duplicated_
  and not _renamed_ — the two claims AXF-129 owns.
- **PENDENCY — `AXF_OBJ_InternalTransfer__c` does not exist in this branch.** The AC states that
  AXF-32's contracts "remain intact", which was read as "do not touch or rename them", not "implement
  them". No test asserts the object's absence, because such a test would fail the day AXF-32 lands.
- **RESIDUAL — the guard cannot see a field that does not exist yet.** It refuses an amount, not a
  future column. The column is covered instead by the vocabulary assertion in the test, which fails if
  a debt-shaped field, question type or view member is later introduced; a ratified §5 amendment that
  adds a legitimate member will have to update that assertion, which is the intended behaviour of a
  contract test.
