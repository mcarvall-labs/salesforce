- (decision) Core decision hierarchy confirmed, in priority order: (1) how much the user can spend, (2) how much the user can invest, (3) how much must be paid including upcoming and overdue bills, (4) how much is expected to be received, and (5) whether obligations can be paid over the coming months.
- (decision) Forecast horizon will be selectable with 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, and 24 month views. The primary expected views are 3, 6, 9, and 12 months. View selection must not truncate longer-lived obligations.
- (decision) Safety reserve policy must be user-configurable among a fixed amount, a percentage of income, or a number of months of expenses. Planned obligations and the reserve are deducted before presenting safe spending or investment capacity.
- (decision) Spending capacity and investment capacity are distinct: spending capacity is the discretionary margin within the plan; investment capacity is only the surplus remaining after obligations, planned discretionary spending, goals, and the safety reserve.
- (discovery) Future revenue includes fixed income such as rent and variable income such as client receipts. Amount behavior (fixed or variable) is distinct from confidence that the receipt will occur.
- (decision) Revenue forecasting is configured per income source or contract. Supported models must include fixed, manually variable, and hourly contractual income. Hourly contracts require currency, hourly rate, expected hours per working day, and working-day rules; multiple contracts with distinct rules and currencies coexist.
- (discovery) Revenue projections are source- and contract-driven. Examples: EUR 28/hour for 8 hours per weekday; BRL 150/hour for 4 hours per weekday; monthly rent that is nearly fixed but has temporary fees in early months. Model needs currency, hourly rate, work schedule, fixed/variable alternatives, temporary deductions, and contract exceptions.
- (decision) Hourly revenue forecasts must calculate each month from its actual working-day calendar and allow holidays, vacation, other non-working days, and manual schedule overrides.
- (decision) Hourly contract forecasts use a contract-specific work calendar. Example: Portugal national holidays for the EUR contract and Brazil national holidays for the BRL contract. Vacation and other non-working days can be excluded manually.

- (decision) Personal and company finances must be separate contexts with their own accounts, cards, transactions, plans, documents, forecasts, reserves, and indicators. An optional consolidated view is required, but it preserves context and does not imply cross-context settlement.
- (decision) Axon manages a household with multiple people and one or more related companies. Example: Michel and Gisele each have personal revenues/expenses and a company. Each entity is separate, with selectable household, person-plus-company, and full family/business consolidation; all consolidated values retain source attribution.
- (decision) Inter-entity transfers are linked on both sides and eliminated from consolidated income/expense to prevent double counting. Account ownership differs from transaction attribution: Michel company uses dedicated PJ account; Gisele company uses her personal Sicoob account, so mixed-use accounts require transaction-level personal/company attribution.
- (scope) Initial scope does not require splitting one transaction between personal and company entities. Each transaction is fully attributed to one entity; partial allocation is a future extension.
